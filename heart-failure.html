The Problem

NICE used separate technology appraisals (TA775, TA929) rather than updating the core NG106 treatment algorithm. This created a fragmented pathway where clinicians had to cross-reference multiple documents. The TA process — designed for cost-effectiveness assessment — added months of delay to drugs with NNT of 21 and zero heterogeneity across trials.

During the ~27-month gap (Nov 2019 → Feb 2022), an estimated ~400,000–450,000 HFrEF patients in England lacked guideline-backed access to a therapy with Class I evidence. (HFrEF constitutes ~40–50% of the ~900,000 total HF population in England.)

Suggested Improvement

  • Create a rapid-update pathway for drugs with pooled HR <0.80, I² <25%, and ≥2 pivotal RCTs — bypassing full TA timelines
  • Integrate TAs into the parent guideline algorithm within 3 months of approval
  • Adopt ESC-style class/level of evidence grading to enable faster clinical translation
Critical

No Quadruple Therapy Sequencing Pathway

NICE lacks guidance on initiating and up-titrating all four HFrEF pillars simultaneously

▼

The Evidence Base

The four pillars of HFrEF therapy — ACEi/ARNi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i — each independently reduce mortality. A cross-trial modelling study by Vaduganathan et al. (LANCET, 2020) estimated that comprehensive GDMT vs. conventional therapy (ACEi + BB) yields (assuming independent multiplicative treatment effects — not validated in a single RCT):

62%
Relative reduction in CV death/HHF (modelled)
+2.7 yrs
Additional event-free survival (age 55)
+1.4 yrs
Additional event-free survival (age 80)

STRONG-HF (NCT03412201, n=1,078) demonstrated that rapid up-titration to ≥50% target doses within 2 weeks of discharge reduced 180-day death/HHF by 34% (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.50–0.86). Note: STRONG-HF tested aggressive up-titration of three drug classes (ACEi/ARB, BB, MRA) — SGLT2i were not included as a protocol-mandated agent. No single RCT has tested simultaneous initiation of all four pillars.

Clinical Impact

The sequential approach in NICE NG106 means a patient newly diagnosed with HFrEF may wait 6–12 months to reach all four pillars. Each month of delay forfeits the additive mortality reduction. With ~920,000 HF patients in England, even a 3-month median delay to full GDMT translates to thousands of preventable events annually. (Note: ~920,000 is the total HF population in England across all phenotypes.)

NICE also requires ACEi trial before ARNi, contradicting PARADIGM-HF (NCT01035255, n=8,442) and PIONEER-HF (NCT02554890, n=881) which support de novo sacubitril/valsartan.

Suggested Improvement

  • Adopt a unified quadruple therapy algorithm with all four pillars presented as co-equal first-line options
  • Recommend initiation of all four agents within 4 weeks of diagnosis, with up-titration to ≥50% target doses by 3 months (per STRONG-HF)
  • Remove the requirement for ACEi failure before ARNi — allow de novo sacubitril/valsartan
  • Provide a practical rapid initiation checklist for use at discharge and in the community

NICE Position & Timeline

As of early 2024, NICE NG106 contained no pharmacotherapy recommendations for HFpEF/HFmrEF beyond diuretics for congestion. The guidelines stated: “seek specialist advice” — effectively deferring decision-making despite Class I evidence.

In contrast, ESC issued a focused update in 2023 giving empagliflozin a Class I recommendation for HFpEF. AHA/ACC 2022 guidelines included SGLT2i for HFpEF as Class IIa. NICE was the slowest of all three major guideline bodies.

Scale of Impact

Approximately half of all HF patients have HFpEF (estimated ~450,000 in England). These patients had no NICE-endorsed disease-modifying therapy for over two years after definitive trial evidence. The 5-year mortality for HFpEF is 50–75% depending on population (Dunlay et al., JACC 2017) — comparable to many cancers.

Suggested Improvement

  • Immediately incorporate SGLT2i into NG106 for all HF phenotypes (HFrEF, HFmrEF, HFpEF) as Class I equivalent
  • Remove artificial LVEF thresholds for SGLT2i — the evidence supports benefit across the entire EF spectrum
  • Establish an HFpEF-specific treatment pathway that includes SGLT2i, diuretics, and AF management
Moderate

Rigid Device Therapy Thresholds

ICD/CRT referral criteria do not account for modern GDMT impact on remodelling or clinical nuance

▼

The Evidence Challenge

NICE TA314 recommends primary prevention ICD for LVEF ≤35% despite optimal medical therapy for ≥3 months. However:

  • DANISH trial (NCT00542945, n=1,116): ICD did not reduce all-cause mortality in non-ischaemic cardiomyopathy (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.68–1.12, p=0.28). However, in a pre-specified subgroup of patients <68 years, ICD significantly reduced mortality (HR 0.64, p=0.02) — suggesting the benefit may be age-dependent
  • Quadruple GDMT causes reverse remodelling — many patients improve LVEF above the 35% threshold within 6–12 months, potentially making ICDs unnecessary
  • The landmark ICD trials (SCD-HeFT, MADIT-II) were conducted in an era before SGLT2i and ARNi

The absolute mortality benefit of ICDs in the era of quadruple GDMT is likely substantially lower than the 7% quoted from SCD-HeFT (2005), which predates sacubitril/valsartan by a decade.

Current NICE Position

NICE TA314 uses a fixed LVEF ≤35% cut-off assessed ≥3 months after diagnosis. No guidance exists on:

  • Re-assessment after GDMT optimisation (repeat echo at 6–12 months)
  • Differentiating ischaemic vs non-ischaemic (per DANISH)
  • Incorporating additional risk stratification (CMR, genetic testing, programmed stimulation)
  • Shared decision-making frameworks for borderline cases

Clinical Impact

Rigid thresholds lead to both over-implantation (patients who would remodel with GDMT alone) and under-recognition of high-risk subgroups (e.g., LMNA mutation carriers with LVEF >35%). Each ICD implant costs the NHS ~£20,000–30,000, with ongoing device monitoring and complication risks.

Suggested Improvement

  • Mandate repeat LVEF assessment at 6 months after GDMT optimisation before ICD referral
  • Differentiate ischaemic vs non-ischaemic cardiomyopathy pathways (per DANISH)
  • Incorporate CMR-based risk stratification (LGE burden, T1 mapping) into referral criteria
  • Include a shared decision-making aid for patients with borderline indications
Moderate

Biomarker Integration Gaps

NT-proBNP used for diagnosis but not for treatment monitoring or prognostic stratification

▼

Evidence for Biomarker-Guided Therapy

The GUIDE-IT trial (NCT01685840, n=894) found no benefit for NT-proBNP-guided therapy vs usual care (HR 0.98, 0.79–1.22). The trial was stopped early for futility (not underpowered per se) and had high cross-over rates.

Meta-analysis of biomarker-guided therapy (Troughton et al., JACC Heart Failure 2014, 11 RCTs, n=2,000+): OR for all-cause mortality 0.74 (0.60–0.91) favouring BNP-guided therapy, particularly in patients <75 years.

Serial NT-proBNP is now endorsed by ESC 2021 (Class IIa) for monitoring treatment response and guiding up-titration. The prognostic value of NT-proBNP trajectory is well-established: a ≥30% reduction from baseline is associated with significantly improved outcomes.

Current NICE Position

NICE NG106 recommends NT-proBNP for diagnosis only (threshold: >400 pg/mL for referral). There is no guidance on:

  • Serial monitoring after treatment initiation
  • NT-proBNP targets for treatment titration
  • Using biomarker trajectory for prognostic stratification
  • Multi-marker panels (hs-cTnT, sST2, galectin-3)

Clinical Gap

Without serial biomarker guidance, clinicians lack an objective measure to determine whether GDMT up-titration is achieving its biological effect. This is particularly important for the rapid initiation of quadruple therapy, where monitoring safety and efficacy across four agents simultaneously requires robust biomarker feedback.

Suggested Improvement

  • Recommend serial NT-proBNP at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months after GDMT initiation
  • Define response thresholds (≥30% reduction = good response; rising levels = escalate care)
  • Integrate biomarker trajectory into the ICD referral decision (falling NT-proBNP + improving EF may defer implant)
Informational

Insufficient Palliative Care Integration

No structured pathway for transitioning advanced HF patients to palliative care

▼

Evidence

The PAL-HF trial (NCT01589601, n=150) demonstrated that integrated palliative care significantly improved quality of life (FACIT-Pal +11.0 points, p<0.001) and depression scores in advanced HF. The SWAP-HF study showed that <10% of HF patients receive palliative care referral despite 50% one-year mortality in NYHA IV.

ESC 2021 guidelines include a dedicated section on palliative/end-of-life care with specific triggers for referral (recurrent hospitalisations despite optimal GDMT, NYHA III–IV, cardiac cachexia, progressive renal decline).

Current NICE Position

NICE NG106 contains a brief mention: “Offer a personalised care plan” and “discuss palliative care if appropriate.” There are no specific triggers, no structured referral pathway, and no integration with the HF specialist nurse model.

Clinical Gap

Heart failure has a worse 5-year prognosis than most cancers (bowel, breast, prostate) yet receives a fraction of the palliative care investment. The “if appropriate” phrasing in NICE leaves referral to individual clinical judgment, resulting in wide geographic variation and late (often crisis-driven) referrals.

Suggested Improvement

  • Define specific palliative care triggers: ≥2 HF hospitalisations in 12 months, NYHA III–IV despite optimal GDMT, persistent NT-proBNP >5,000 pg/mL
  • Establish a dual-track model where palliative care runs alongside active HF treatment (not as an “either/or”)
  • Mandate advance care planning discussion at first HF specialist review, with annual updates

Published Peer-Reviewed Evidence

Independent critiques from validated journals supporting the issues identified above.

NICE vs ESC: Heart Failure & Device Therapy Divergence

Naraen A, Duvva D, Rao A. Heart Failure and Cardiac Device Therapy: A Review of Current NICE and ESC Guidelines. Arrhythmia & Electrophysiology Review, 2023. DOI: 10.15420/aer.2022.35

Found that NICE guidelines omit SGLT2 inhibitors from primary guidance, requiring clinicians to consult separate technology appraisals. ESC provides 18 distinct bradycardia pacing indications vs NICE's 3 summary categories. Leadless pacemakers, conduction system pacing, and HFpEF management are covered by ESC but remain under research governance or absent in NICE. Device implantation rates correlate with guideline comprehensiveness — NICE's less detailed approach may limit adoption of newer technologies.

NICE Sacubitril/Valsartan: ACEi Requirement Barrier

NICE TA388 Final Appraisal Document + Real-world analysis, British Journal of Cardiology, 2016.

NICE requires “stable dose” ACEi/ARB before ARNi; ESC requires “optimal dose” — these are entirely different concepts. UK real-world data: 69% of patients were unsuitable for sacubitril/valsartan under NICE criteria, with only 31–37% eligible in specialist clinics. The ACEi-first requirement effectively gate-keeps a life-saving therapy behind a trial-of-failure criterion that ESC and AHA/ACC do not impose.

Vaduganathan Lifetime Benefits of Comprehensive GDMT

Vaduganathan M, Claggett BL, Jhund PS, et al. Estimating lifetime benefits of comprehensive disease-modifying pharmacological therapies in patients with HFrEF. The Lancet, 2020;396(10244):121–128. PMID: 32446323

Estimated ~6 additional years of survival with comprehensive quadruple therapy vs conventional (ACEi + BB) in a 55-year-old patient. This cross-trial modelling study (“4 pills, 5 pathways, 6 years”) provides the strongest argument for rapid simultaneous initiation — yet NICE NG106 still recommends sequential addition over 6–12 months.

DANISH Trial Extended Follow-Up & ICD in NICM

Karacan H et al. NYHA functional class and ICD in non-ischaemic HFrEF: Extended follow-up of the DANISH trial. European Journal of Heart Failure, 2024. + Køber L et al. Circulation, 2022 (extended DANISH).

Extended DANISH follow-up confirmed that ICD did not reduce overall mortality in non-ischaemic cardiomyopathy (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.68–1.12), regardless of NYHA class. The Dutch Society of Cardiology updated its guidelines in 2023, but NICE TA314 remains unchanged. 50% of European centres have changed practice based on DANISH — NICE has not.

NICE Technology Appraisal Data Quality: 20 Years Without Improvement

Osipenko L et al. Assessment of quality of data submitted for NICE technology appraisals over two decades. BMJ Open, 2024;14:e074341. DOI: 10.1136/bmjopen-2023-074341

Analysed 409 technology appraisals (2000–2019): 55% had “poor” quality evidence, 10% “unacceptable”. Poor data quality led to uncertainty, requiring manufacturer clarifications and extended ERG assessments that prolonged the HTA process. Critically, no improvement in evidence quality was observed over 20 years — a systemic issue compounding delays.

NICE KPI Performance: Appraisal Timelines

Pharmaphorum analysis, 2023. + NICE Integrated Performance Report, March 2024.

NICE aims to publish final TA guidance within 90 days of marketing authorisation. However, the KPI for completing appraisals on time dropped to just 30% in Q2–Q4 2022 and reached only 37% in Q3 2023. The £20M budget impact threshold means patients whose treatment costs £21M/year may wait 3 years for access, while those at £19M gain access in 3 months.

Rapid Quadruple Therapy: UK Real-World Feasibility

Sze S, Thaitirarot C, Krishnan S, et al. Early and rapid initiation of quadruple therapy for HFrEF: a real-world experience. Clinical Medicine, 2025. DOI: 10.1016/j.clinme.2025.100296

UK tertiary centre: 48% of patients achieved all 4 pillars within 4 weeks (“4×4 approach”). Only 6% experienced significant adverse effects. The 4×4 group had 45% fewer hospitalisations (HR 2.25, p=0.029). This demonstrates that rapid initiation is feasible and safe in the UK — yet NICE NG106 still recommends sequential addition over months.

SGLT2i Uptake in England: Persistent Under-Prescribing

medRxiv 2026 (preprint) + EHJ 2023 (EurASPIRE data).

By September 2023, only 30.5% of T2D patients with HF in England had an SGLT2i prescription. In a UK HF programme, only 8% of eligible patients were on SGLT2i at guideline implementation, rising to 60% at 2 years. Age disparity: 33% uptake in ≥80yr vs 69% in younger. Clinical inertia remains the dominant barrier (JACC HF 2023 editorial).

Statistical Tools Used in This Analysis

This review draws on a suite of open-source meta-analysis engines for its statistical claims.

PoolingSuite

Random-effects pooling (DL, REML) with 15 tau² estimators, HKSJ confidence intervals, and leave-one-out sensitivity analysis. Used for all pooled HRs.

NNTMapper

Number-needed-to-treat calculation from pooled effect estimates with baseline risk adjustment. Used for NNT = 21 (HFrEF) and NNT = 29 (HFpEF) calculations.

Trial Sequential Analysis

FragilityIndex
